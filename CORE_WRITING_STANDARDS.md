# Shared Professional Research & Writing Standards

These standards apply to all skills. Individual skill files repeat the key requirements so each skill remains usable when installed independently.

## Quality principles
- Write for the real audience, goal, context, and constraints.
- Sound like a thoughtful, competent human writer: clear, specific, credible, natural, and appropriately formal.
- Vary sentence length and structure without ornament.
- Prefer evidence, concrete examples, and precise verbs over adjectives and self-praise.
- Remove filler, clichés, boilerplate, generic praise, redundancy, artificial transitions, and unnecessary jargon.
- Preserve the user's voice and meaning. Do not invent a persona or experience.
- Use placeholders or ask targeted questions for missing facts.
- Distinguish verified facts, user-provided facts, interpretation, assumptions, and recommendations.

## Research integrity
- Prioritize primary and official sources.
- Verify citation metadata and source claims. Never invent citations, DOIs, titles, quotations, statistics, findings, or URLs.
- Mark unverified sources and facts clearly.
- Avoid claiming novelty, exhaustive coverage, or causal conclusions beyond the evidence.
- Explain limitations, uncertainty, and methodological risks.

## Application integrity
- Do not invent credentials, grades, awards, publications, responsibilities, results, or endorsements.
- Tailor documents using verified opportunity details.
- Applicants must review and own their application materials and follow relevant AI-use policies.
- Recommenders must independently assess, approve, and submit/sign recommendation letters.

## Final prose review
1. Does every paragraph serve the reader and prompt?
2. Are claims supported by examples or evidence?
3. Are repeated ideas removed?
4. Does it sound natural rather than formulaic?
5. Is the tone appropriate and credible?
6. Is the voice consistent with the author's actual profile?
7. Are facts, citations, dates, and program details verified?
