# CV Creation, Review, Tailoring, and ATS

## Document Type

Identify academic CV, research CV, professional resume, teaching CV, or
a required institutional template. Follow the target's explicit
instructions over generic conventions.

## Section Selection

Select sections based on opportunity and evidence. Possible sections
include contact, research interests, education, research experience,
projects, publications, presentations, awards, teaching, employment,
service, technical skills, languages, and referees when requested. Omit
empty or irrelevant sections unless required.

## Tailoring

Maintain a master profile and create separate versions for separate
opportunities. Map each requirement to actual evidence. Reorder
sections, select relevant projects, and improve wording without changing
facts.

## Bullet Writing

Prefer action + task/contribution + method/tool + verified outcome where
available. Do not force a metric when none exists. Distinguish
individual and team work.

## ATS

-   Separate required and preferred criteria.
-   Extract relevant terms and concepts from the job description.
-   Match keywords to evidence, not merely to word presence.
-   Classify found, partial, no evidence, or needs clarification.
-   Ask whether the user has relevant experience before concluding a
    skill gap.
-   Use standard headings and readable text structure.
-   Check extraction order, dates, links, tables, columns, graphics, and
    file format where possible.
-   Do not promise ATS success or claim a universal ATS score.

## Review

Check completeness, accuracy, chronology, consistency, relevance,
grammar, spelling, unsupported claims, layout, links, and requested
length/format.

## Output

Provide the requested CV or resume, missing details, tailored section
plan, and relevant quality findings. Do not force a full profile report
for a simple CV edit.
